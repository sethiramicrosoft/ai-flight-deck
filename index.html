Understand what the scan did and did not check

This assessment combines collected Microsoft 365 settings, imported reports, and statements from responsible people. These are called evidence. Each readiness check, also called a control, compares that information with a rule defined by Flight Deck. Open a service area below to see the rule, the information used, what is missing, and the next step.

A successful connection means the app could read a service, not that all its checks passed. This version supports automatic validation for 3 checks and owner statements for 11 checks; 63 of the 77 checks do not yet have a supported validation method. Missing information does not prove that your organization is incorrectly configured. The AFD identifiers belong to this app, not a Microsoft certification standard.

How to interpret sharing counts, scores, and downloads

A displayed sharing score starts at 100 and subtracts points under the app's sharing rules; it is not a percentage of Copilot readiness. Missing required scan information can make that score unavailable. Review-priority counts describe records flagged for attention, not proven incidents. Permission counts are not unique-file counts, and an audience estimate is not a count of people who actually opened an item.

The review-summary CSV describes grouped results. The collected-permission CSV contains the underlying records from the scan, not all permissions in Microsoft 365. The readiness-check CSV records the app's check results and supporting information. Downloading these files does not send them to administrators; review them before sharing through your organization's approved process.

Step 1 of 4

Start by reading settings from your Microsoft 365 organization

Your Microsoft 365 organization is called a tenant. Connect with an approved administrator account so Flight Deck can read available settings, permissions, and reports. The first saved scan is called the baseline. After it loads, choose the people for your pilot and record the settings and decisions below. The app uses this information to explain what has been checked and what still needs attention.

What happens when you connect and scan?

The app uses Microsoft Graph to read available organization information and also attempts to connect to Exchange Online, SharePoint Online, Microsoft Purview, and Power Platform / Copilot Studio. These service-specific readers are called collectors. Missing connector software may be installed for your Windows user, and scan results are saved locally. You do not need to assemble report packages for the normal scan.

Complete Microsoft sign-in, multifactor authentication, and permission prompts for each service when requested. One successful sign-in does not grant access to every service. If a connection fails, read its message below and use the connection guide to identify the administrator role or permission required. Collected settings are not automatically accepted as passed checks: this version validates 3 checks automatically, supports 11 owner-statement checks, and cannot validate the other 63 checks.

      Read the meaning before deciding what to do. A missing result is different from a failed requirement, and a successful sharing comparison is different from overall pilot readiness.

      What each stage is for
      Use the four stages as one connected decision process.
      
        
      
        
      
          
      1. Set up your pilot
      Sign in and collect the first scan, choose pilot participants, and record decisions. Add existing reports if needed. Use the outstanding-check list to identify your next action.
      
          
      2. Review assessment
      Open each of the 13 service areas to see which requirements were assessed and which remain unknown. Inspect sharing records before treating broad access as a problem.
      
          
      3. Plan corrections
      Select proposed actions and download a review file. Administrators must approve and implement any changes outside the app; selecting an action does not fix anything.
      
          
      4. Review pilot decision
      Read the recommendation, unresolved requirements, and administrator instructions. Compare a later scan after approved changes. An improvement in sharing alone does not establish overall readiness.
